# Great Readings

This collection brings together seminal monographs, paradigm-defining essays, and landmark theoretical papers in Library and Information Studies (LIS). Rather than introductory textbooks, these works represent milestone breakthroughs that have shaped how we conceptualize information, knowledge organization, human sensemaking, and cultural memory.

## Philosophical Foundations and Disciplinary Paradigms

Foundational texts that defined the social mission, philosophy, and ontological nature of information and librarianship.

| Title | Author(s) | Type | Why It's Worth Reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [*The Five Laws of Library Science*](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.283188) | S. R. Ranganathan | Classic Monograph | Articulates the five enduring normative principles that transformed libraries from static storehouses into dynamic, user-centered institutions. |
| [*As We May Think*](https://www.theatlantic.com/magazine/archive/1945/07/as-we-may-think/303881/) | Vannevar Bush | Seminal Essay | Conceptualizes the "Memex," introducing associative indexing and anticipating hypertext, digital repositories, and personal information retrieval. |
| [*Information as Thing*](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/5400b22s) | Michael K. Buckland | Landmark Paper | Clarifies information ontology by distinguishing information-as-process, information-as-knowledge, and information-as-thing—the physical and digital artifacts handled by retrieval systems. |
| *The Foundations of Education for Librarianship* | Jesse H. Shera | Landmark Monograph | Formulates the concept of "social epistemology," arguing that librarianship must study how society produces, organizes, and integrates collective knowledge. |
| *The Atlas of New Librarianship* | R. David Lankes | Monograph | Redefines librarianship through conversation theory, arguing that the true mission of libraries is facilitating knowledge creation rather than managing physical inventories. |

## Classification, Power, and Critical Information Studies

Works exploring how bibliographic systems, taxonomies, and automated data structures encode social values, power dynamics, and structural biases.

| Title | Author(s) | Type | Why It's Worth Reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *Sorting Things Out: Classification and Its Consequences* | Geoffrey C. Bowker and Susan Leigh Star | Landmark Monograph | Demonstrates the invisible moral, political, and material power embedded in classification systems, standards, and categorization schemes. |
| *Prejudices and Antipathies: A Tract on the LC Subject Heads Concerning People* | Sanford Berman | Landmark Monograph | A pioneering activist critique that methodically exposed Eurocentric, racist, and patriarchal biases embedded in standardized bibliographic vocabularies. |
| *The Power to Name: Locating the Limits of Subject Representation in Modern Classification* | Hope A. Olson | Monograph | Deconstructs the universalist assumptions of Dewey and Library of Congress classification, showing how structural standards systematically marginalize alternative ways of knowing. |
| *Algorithms of Oppression: How Search Engines Reinforce Racism* | Safiya Umoja Noble | Monograph | Investigates how commercial search engine algorithms and automated information curation reflect and amplify racial and gender biases. |
| *Race After Technology: Abolitionist Tools for the New Jim Code* | Ruha Benjamin | Monograph | Analyzes the intersection of race, design, and computational systems, offering critical concepts for dismantling discriminatory information architectures. |

## Human Information Interaction and Sensemaking

Pioneering empirical and cognitive models analyzing how people experience, formulate, search for, and use information.

| Title | Author(s) | Type | Why It's Worth Reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *Question-Negotiation and Information Seeking in Libraries* | Robert S. Taylor | Landmark Paper | Identifies the four hierarchical levels of human information need (visceral, conscious, formalized, compromised) during the reference interaction. |
| *The Design of Browsing and Berrypicking Techniques for the Online Search Interface* | Marcia J. Bates | Landmark Paper | Overturns the static single-query retrieval model by introducing the non-linear "berrypicking" model that mirrors real human search trajectories. |
| *Seeking Meaning: A Process Approach to Library and Information Services* | Carol C. Kuhlthau | Landmark Monograph | Formulates the Information Search Process (ISP) model, mapping the affective phases of uncertainty, anxiety, and clarity that accompany complex research. |
| *From the Mind's Eye of the User: The Sense-Making Approach* | Brenda Dervin | Landmark Paper | Introduces the Sense-Making methodology, conceptualizing information seeking as a universal cognitive strategy to bridge situational knowledge gaps. |
| *Anomalous States of Knowledge as a Basis for Information Retrieval* | Nicholas J. Belkin | Landmark Paper | Proposes that information retrieval systems should focus on addressing a user's recognized knowledge anomaly rather than relying solely on keyword matching. |

## Retrieval Systems, Network Architecture, and Document Theory

Theoretical and structural foundations governing how information is bounded, structured, indexed, and navigated across digital networks.

| Title | Author(s) | Type | Why It's Worth Reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *What is a Document? (Qu'est-ce que la documentation?)* | Suzanne Briet | Classic Treatise | Establishes modern document theory by arguing that any physical or recorded object functions as a document once it is indexed and curated as evidence. |
| *Libraries of the Future* | J. C. R. Licklider | Visionary Monograph | A seminal mid-century work forecasting the shift from paper collections to distributed, interactive computing networks and knowledge-base systems. |
| *Relevance: A Review of the Literature and a Framework for Thinking on the Notion in Information Science* | Tefko Saracevic | Landmark Paper | Synthesizes decades of theoretical literature into a comprehensive stratified framework defining systems, cognitive, situational, and affective relevance. |
| *Information Architecture: For the Web and Beyond* | Louis Rosenfeld, Peter Morville, and Jorge Arango | Landmark Monograph | The canonical blueprint for structural design, labeling, taxonomy creation, and navigational findability in digital information ecosystems. |

## Archives, Materiality, and Cultural Memory

Essential treatises on the preservation of digital artifacts, the philosophical nature of archives, and institutional memory.

| Title | Author(s) | Type | Why It's Worth Reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *Archive Fever: A Freudian Impression* | Jacques Derrida | Philosophical Monograph | Explores the institutional impulse to preserve and control recorded memory alongside the structural repression and exclusion inherent in archival authority. |
| *What is Past is Prologue: A History of Archival Ideas Since 1898 and the Future Paradigm Shift* | Terry Cook | Landmark Paper | Chronicles the theoretical evolution of archives across four paradigms, from passive custody of state records to activist societal memory curation. |
| *Mechanisms: New Media and the Forensic Imagination* | Matthew G. Kirschenbaum | Landmark Monograph | Pioneers the application of textual bibliography and digital forensics to electronic media, analyzing the physical and logical realities of digital bits. |
| *The Case for Books: Past, Present, and Future* | Robert Darnton | Essay Collection | Examines the historical trajectory of the printed book, the political economy of publishing, and the imperative to defend the public digital commons against commercial enclosure. |
| *Archiving the Unspeakable: Silence, Memory, and the Photographic Record in Cambodia* | Michelle Caswell | Landmark Monograph | Investigates the evidential and human rights dimensions of records of mass atrocity, arguing for survivor-centered and community-accountable archival ethics. |
