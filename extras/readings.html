Great Readings

This collection brings together seminal monographs, paradigm-defining essays, and landmark theoretical papers in Library and Information Studies (LIS). Rather than introductory textbooks, these works represent milestone breakthroughs that have shaped how we conceptualize information, knowledge organization, human sensemaking, and cultural memory.

Philosophical Foundations and Disciplinary Paradigms

Foundational texts that defined the social mission, philosophy, and ontological nature of information and librarianship.

Title Author(s)
The Five Laws of Library Science S. R. Ranganathan
As We May Think Vannevar Bush
Information as Thing Michael K. Buckland
The Foundations of Education for Librarianship Jesse H. Shera
Our Enduring Values: Librarianship in the 21st Century Michael Gorman
The Atlas of New Librarianship R. David Lankes

Classification, Power, and Critical Information Studies

Works exploring how bibliographic systems, taxonomies, and automated data structures encode social values, power dynamics, and structural biases.

Title Author(s)
Sorting Things Out: Classification and Its Consequences Geoffrey C. Bowker and Susan Leigh Star
Prejudices and Antipathies: A Tract on the LC Subject Heads Concerning People Sanford Berman
The Power to Name: Locating the Limits of Subject Representation in Modern Classification Hope A. Olson
Algorithms of Oppression: How Search Engines Reinforce Racism Safiya Umoja Noble
Race After Technology: Abolitionist Tools for the New Jim Code Ruha Benjamin
Book Banning in 21st-Century America Emily J. M. Knox

Human Information Interaction and Sensemaking

Pioneering empirical and cognitive models analyzing how people experience, formulate, search for, and use information.

Title Author(s)
Question-Negotiation and Information Seeking in Libraries Robert S. Taylor
The Design of Browsing and Berrypicking Techniques for the Online Search Interface Marcia J. Bates
Seeking Meaning: A Process Approach to Library and Information Services Carol C. Kuhlthau
Models in Information Behaviour Research T. D. Wilson
Information Needs and Uses Brenda Dervin and Michael Nilan
ASK for Information Retrieval: Part I. Background and Theory Nicholas J. Belkin, Robert N. Oddy, and H. M. Brooks

Retrieval Systems, Network Architecture, and Document Theory

Theoretical and structural foundations governing how information is bounded, structured, indexed, and navigated across digital networks.

Title Author(s)
Traité de documentation : le livre sur le livre, théorie et pratique Paul Otlet
What is Documentation? Suzanne Briet (translated by Ronald E. Day and Laurent Martinet)
What is a "document"? Michael K. Buckland
A Mathematical Theory of Communication Claude E. Shannon
Libraries of the Future J. C. R. Licklider
Relevance: A Review of the Literature and a Framework for Thinking on the Notion in Information Science Tefko Saracevic

Archives, Materiality, and Cultural Memory

Essential treatises on the preservation of digital artifacts, the philosophical nature of archives, and institutional memory.

Title Author(s)

The Case for Books: Past, Present, and Future Robert Darnton
Archiving the Unspeakable: Silence, Memory, and the Photographic Record in Cambodia Michelle Caswell
